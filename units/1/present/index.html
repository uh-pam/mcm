Rational and irrational numbers

A real number is rational if it can be written as a fraction of integers and irrational otherwise. A second, independent classification cuts across this one.

A real (or complex) number is algebraic if it is a root of some nonzero polynomial with integer coefficients, and transcendental otherwise.

Every rational is algebraic (p/qp/qp/q is a root of qx−pqx-pqx−p), and so are some irrationals: 2\sqrt22​ is a root of x2−2x^2-2x2−2. Numbers such as π\piπ and e\mathrm{e}e are transcendental, the root of no integer polynomial, and so they are irrational too. The next example shows the standard way to prove a number irrational.

Suppose, for contradiction, that 2=pq\sqrt2=\tfrac pq2​=qp​ with p,q∈Np,q\in\mathbb{N}p,q∈N sharing no common factor. Squaring gives p2=2q2p^2=2q^2p2=2q2, so p2p^2p2 is even, hence ppp is even (the square of an odd number is odd), say p=2kp=2kp=2k. Then 4k2=2q24k^2=2q^24k2=2q2, i.e. q2=2k2q^2=2k^2q2=2k2, so q2q^2q2 is even and qqq is even too. But then ppp and qqq share the factor 222, contradicting our assumption. Therefore no such fraction exists and 2\sqrt22​ is irrational.

The logistic (sigmoid) function

The logistic (or sigmoid) function is not piecewise: it is a single smooth formula. It is a smooth cousin of the step HHH and one of the most important functions in data science.

σ(x)=11+e−x.\sigma(x)=\frac{1}{1+\mathrm{e}^{-x}}.σ(x)=1+e−x1​.
